Reflection

Written after running the three tickets. Everything below can be checked against the Ticket 101, Ticket 102, Ticket 103 and Cash tabs on this page. The shop started with $3,400.00 and ended with $160.00. All three tickets are closed, but only #102 ended with somebody getting what they asked for.

1 How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket, and why?

#101 Bulldog tee — found the real problem, then forgot the customer

Tauhid asked for one white tee in size S. The shop has ten white tees, none of them size S. The Ticket 101 tab shows Inventory checking the exact size and finding zero. The whole ticket turns on that check — an agent that asked only "do we have white tees?" would have said yes and promised a shirt that does not exist.

Then the second problem: the only printer who makes this shirt refuses to send anything while an old bill of $840.00 sits unpaid. Accounting did the best thing in the whole run here. Before recommending the $840.00 it looked up the rent also coming due, to check the shop could afford both — you can see it on the Ticket 101 tab, reading the lease on a ticket that has nothing to do with rent.

What the owner was asked to decide: one request, $840.00 to settle the bill. The agents could not pay it themselves. Purnima Prasad approved it and only then did the money leave — the Cash tab shows the balance fall from $3,400.00 to $2,560.00. Had she declined, nothing would have moved and the ticket would have been refused.

The failure: nobody wrote to Tauhid. The shop spent $840.00 partly on his behalf and sent him not one word. There was a tool for writing to customers. It was not used.

To be fair: the tee was never reaching Tauhid that day anyway. The next step was ordering the reprint, and I never built them a tool for ordering from a supplier. That gap is mine. The ticket says resolved because the shop ran out of things it could do, not because anyone got a shirt.

#102 Rent — the best work of the three

The landlord's email said "rent due in 2 days". The Ticket 102 tab shows Facilities not taking that at face value: it opened the lease, confirmed $2,400.00 due 2026-09-02, and reported the rent as coming but not yet late. It did not simply believe the person who was owed the money.

Accounting confirmed the shop could afford it and raised one request for $2,400.00, paying nothing until a person signed. Purnima Prasad approved it. The Cash tab shows the result: $2,400.00 out, $2,560.00 down to $160.00. This is the only ticket where closing it also means the job is genuinely done.

Why this one worked: everything it needed was something the shop could actually do. Paying rent requires no goods to exist.

#103 Discount — good business sense, poor customer care

The Yale AI Club asked for 20 navy hoodies at a discount. The Ticket 103 tab shows Customer Service looking up what the shop paid for a hoodie ($22.00) before naming any price. That is the right order — without it you can easily talk yourself into a discount that loses money on every sale. It suggested $46.00 against the normal $58.00, which still leaves the shop $24.00 a hoodie.

What the owner was asked to decide: sell at $46.00 a hoodie instead of $58.00. Purnima Prasad approved it and it moved $0.00, because agreeing a price is not a purchase. The Cash tab shows this line at zero while the other two show money leaving. The normal $58.00 price was left alone for everyone else.

The failure: only 8 hoodies exist, not 20, and the price still went to the owner for all 20. Customer Service did write the shortfall down — the draft on the Ticket 103 tab says 8 in stock, 12 not, no restock date. But it was written before the decision, so it says the discount is "not approved yet", and nothing was written afterwards. The club has a price it has not been told about, and 12 hoodies with no date.

Overall — the three decisions: ticket 101, $840.00 approved and paid; ticket 102, $2,400.00 approved and paid; ticket 103, a $46.00 price approved with no money moved. One decision per ticket, every one taken by a person. On money I cannot fault them — the Cash tab shows they paid for nothing themselves and the figures add up exactly. They fell down on people: the club got a draft that was never updated, and Tauhid got nothing at all.

Every hand-off starts a whole second conversation. The other agent has to be told the situation from scratch, look things up, think, and report back, and all that reading and writing is what you pay for. The Boss already has access to every tool the specialists have, so on these three tickets asking a specialist bought no new ability — only extra time and cost.

One agent would also have been simpler to build. Several of the worst bugs here existed only because there were five. A spending limit meant for one agent got applied to the whole team and stopped every run. The Boss crashed whenever a specialist asked it a question back. And an agent that was busy working showed on screen as "not called".

The thing that actually matters: the five agents are not what keeps the money safe. The tools do that: no payment without a signature, each signature good once and only for that bill, amounts read from the records rather than typed by the AI, and anything that would overdraw refused. One agent using those same tools would be exactly as safe.

What five agents did buy is real but small. Each only had to learn its own rules, and you can see who reached which conclusion, which is why the ticket tabs read as they do. That makes the work easier to explain without making it any more correct. Five agents made a better story of the work; one agent would have been a better machine for doing it.

4 Three new problems this team could solve with the tools already built

A. A customer order the shop can actually fill

A student orders a blue cap. Unlike the size S tee on Ticket 101, the shop has 40 of them. Every tool needed already exists: Inventory can check the exact size, Customer Service can check the price, there is a tool that takes items off the shelf (and refuses to take more than there are), another that writes the confirmation, and another that closes the ticket.

This is the case the board never got to show. Only #102 ended with anyone actually receiving what they asked for. A cap order would close and be delivered, and needs nothing new.

B. A second supplier stops shipping over an unpaid bill

Elm City Gifts, who supply the mugs, send a bill that goes unpaid — and the shop has zero crest mugs left. The team can check whether that supplier is blocked, read what is owed and whether it is late, put the payment in front of the owner, and pay it once signed, which frees the supplier to ship again.

This is the same shape of problem as Ticket 101, with a different supplier and product. The team solved it once; the tools work again without changes.

C. Two bills arrive and the money only covers one

The team can read the balance, read each bill and its due date, see what has already been paid, lay out which one matters most, and ask the owner to approve one at a time. If anything would take the account below zero, the tool refuses it rather than trusting the AI to notice.

It nearly happened on this very run. The Cash tab shows the two bills totalled $3,240.00 against $3,400.00 — payable together, but with only $160.00 to spare. A slightly larger invoice and the shop would have had to choose.

5 Three new problems this team could not solve — and what it would take

A. Actually ordering the reprint

Why it fails today: this is the wall Ticket 101 hit. The bill was paid, the printer was willing, and then nothing could happen, because no tool exists for ordering from a supplier. Ticket 101 is only resolved in the sense that the shop ran out of moves — its note still says size S is at 0.

What to store: a list of orders placed (supplier, item, size, how many, price, date ordered, date expected) and a record of what actually turned up.

Tools needed: one to place an order — which must need a signature first, because placing an order commits the shop's money; one to check where an order has got to; and one to add stock when a delivery arrives. That last one should be the only way items can ever be added, so stock can never appear just because someone says it did.

Agents needed: none new. Inventory already handles suppliers and delivery times; this would let it actually place the order instead of only advising, with Accounting still controlling the spending.

B. Money coming in — sales and refunds

Why it fails today: money only ever goes out here. Every line on the Cash tab is a payment. There is no way to record a sale or refund anyone. The $46.00 price approved on Ticket 103 would have brought in $920.00 if all 20 sold, and the shop could not record a cent of it.

What to store: a record of sales, and a record of refunds linked back to the sale they came from.

Tools needed: one that records a sale — adding the money and removing the items at the same moment, so the two can never disagree — and one that issues a refund, which must need a signature, since it is the easiest tool in the set to misuse.

Agents needed: Accounting would widen from only paying bills to also taking money in, and Customer Service would be allowed to recommend a refund while never being able to give one.

C. Time moving forward

Why it fails today: the shop is frozen on 2026-08-31. Every date on this page comes from that one day. The team can answer "is the rent late today?" — as Facilities did on Ticket 102 — but it cannot answer "what happens on 2 September if nobody pays?", and it cannot see next month's rent coming. Every judgement on this board is a single snapshot.

What to store: a list of bills that repeat (what, how often, next due), and a history of how late each bill has become.

Tools needed: one that moves the shop's date forward — which should never be given to an agent, because an agent that can change the date can make anything late or not late; one that lists what is coming up; and one that chases a bill that has gone properly overdue.

Agents needed: a sixth agent — a Planner — whose job is next week rather than today: what is due, whether the money will stretch, and what to put in front of the owner now so they are not deciding in a panic on the day. None of the current five ever look past today, because nothing in this shop ever has.
